# Interpretation rules

Keep three types of statements separate.

1. Source observation: quote both passages with raw hashes and lines. Example: the target says to wait before implementation; the variant says to implement before asking.
2. Agent interpretation: describe the focused direction of change and what it may imply. The runtime verifies quote integrity; a semantic judgment can still be wrong.
3. User-specific suggestion: provide only when requested and after reading the user's actual Skill. Explain the relevant tradeoff and let the user decide.

Do not infer why authors acted, independent invention, popularity or ancestry from recurring text. Use “observed in the captured corpus” with the engine's exact counts. Never equate a frequent instruction with a good instruction.

Recurrence requires at least three verified YES groups across three representative repositories, with no repository above half the supporting groups and no more than 20% NO/UNCERTAIN decisions. These are operational guardrails, not independence tests.

A preserved instruction can be UNCHANGED even when its words or location changed. Conversely, similar wording can reverse behavior. For ADDED/REMOVED claims, search the full relevant source context for an existing equivalent requirement. Local snippets cannot establish global absence.

Unresolved source evidence is a limitation to report, not a negative label. A complete workflow does not establish an exhaustive corpus, correct semantics or measured agent behavior.
